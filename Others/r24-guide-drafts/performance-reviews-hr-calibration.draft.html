Calibration is the last stage of a performance review and the only one that sees every review side by side. Managers rate the people they know; HR is the only party positioned to ask whether a 4 in one team means the same as a 4 in another. This guide covers that stage — what you are deciding, and what finalising actually commits.

What arrives with you

A review reaches you once the manager has submitted, in the state Manager reviewed. By then it carries:

  • the employee's self-assessment — their ratings and comments;
  • the manager's ratings and comments across the same competencies;
  • an attendance and leave summary for the cycle period, generated automatically — days present, late, absent and early out, an attendance percentage, and leave taken.

You are the first person to see all three together, and the only one who sees them across the whole organization.

What calibration is actually for

Not to re-run the review. The manager saw the work; you did not. Calibration is for the things a single manager structurally cannot see:

  • Different standards between managers. One rates generously, another sparingly, and the same performance lands a grade apart. Only someone reading both can tell.
  • Ratings that cluster. A team where everybody is Exceeds Expectations has not distinguished anyone, and neither has a team where everybody Meets.
  • Ratings without evidence. A 5 with no comment is not a finding; it is an assertion.
  • Large gaps between self and manager assessment. These are worth reading rather than averaging. A consistent under-rater and an expectation that was never made explicit look identical in the numbers and are completely different problems.

Setting the final rating

You add HR remarks and an overall rating, then complete the review. The scale is the same one managers used:

  1. Needs Significant Improvement
  2. Below Expectations
  3. Meets Expectations
  4. Exceeds Expectations
  5. Outstanding

Where your rating departs from the manager's, write down why. That remark is the record of the calibration decision, and it is the only thing that will explain the difference to anyone reading the review later — including the manager, who will be asked about it.

Changing a rating without talking to the manager

Avoid it. A manager who discovers at delivery that their rating was altered has been put in an impossible position: they must defend a judgement that is not theirs, to someone who reports to them. If a rating needs to move, the conversation happens before the review is completed, not after.

Completing the review

Completing sets the review to Completed. That is the final state — the review is closed, and the manager can no longer edit their section.

Before completing, check:

  • Every competency is rated, with comments where the rating is at either extreme.
  • The manager's comments are specific enough to be delivered to the employee.
  • Any rating you changed carries an HR remark explaining it.
  • The employee has actually had, or is scheduled to have, the conversation. A completed review that nobody discussed is an administrative record, not a performance process.

Managing review cycles

Cycles are created and configured by Admin and HR. A cycle defines the period a review covers, and that period is what the attendance and leave summaries are calculated from — so the dates are not cosmetic.

Practical points:

  • Set dates to the period being assessed, not the period you are running the process in. A cycle covering January to June, run in July, should have January to June dates or every attendance summary will be wrong.
  • Reviews are created in Draft and wait on the employee. Nobody can start before the cycle exists.
  • Employees who joined mid-cycle will show a short attendance record. That is arithmetic, not performance — make sure managers know before they rate.
  • Competencies and the rating scale are configurable. Change them between cycles rather than during one, or half your reviews will be scored against a different definition from the other half.

Common questions

A manager has not submitted and the cycle is closing

The review stays at Self review submitted and cannot be finalised — the stages are ordered and there is no way to skip one. Chase the manager; the alternative is a review with no managerial input, which is worse than a late one.

Every rating in a team is a 3

Usually a manager avoiding the conversation rather than a genuinely uniform team. It is worth asking directly, because a uniform result is exactly what a review process exists to avoid producing.

Can a completed review be reopened?

Completed is final. Treat completion as the commitment it is, and do the checking beforehand.

The attendance summary looks wrong

It is generated from attendance records for the cycle dates, so check the cycle dates first and the underlying records second — see attendance audit. It cannot be edited on the review itself, by design: the review should reflect the record rather than a corrected version of it.
