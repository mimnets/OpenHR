# Performance Reviews — For Managers

A performance review in OpenHRApp passes through three people. The employee writes a self-assessment, **you** add the manager review, and HR finalises it. This guide covers your stage: what arrives with you, what you are adding, and what happens once you submit.

## Where your stage sits

Every review moves through four states in order:

1. **Draft** — created for the cycle, waiting on the employee.
2. **Self review submitted** — the employee has finished. *This is when it reaches you.*
3. **Manager reviewed** — you have submitted. It is now with HR.
4. **Completed** — HR has finalised it.

Two consequences worth holding onto. You cannot start until the employee has submitted theirs — that ordering is deliberate, so your assessment is informed by their account rather than the reverse. And **your submission is not the end**: HR reviews after you, and their calibration stage is where the final rating is settled. See [HR calibration](/how-to-use/performance-reviews-hr-calibration).

## Finding reviews waiting on you

Open **Performance** from the sidebar. You see reviews for the people you are responsible for — members of teams you lead, and anyone whose line manager is set to you.

If someone is missing, it is almost always that mapping rather than the review cycle. A manager with no team leadership and no direct reports sees no reviews at all.

## What you are rating

Reviews are scored across six competencies:

- **Agility** — adapting to changing priorities and handling change transparently.
- **Collaboration**
- **Customer Focus**
- **Developing Others**
- **Global Mindset**
- **Innovation Mindset**

Each carries a short description and a set of example behaviours — read them rather than rating from the title alone. "Collaboration" means something specific here, and two managers rating from instinct will not mean the same thing by it.

Administrators can change the competency set, so yours may differ.

### The rating scale

1. **Needs Significant Improvement**
2. **Below Expectations**
3. **Meets Expectations**
4. **Exceeds Expectations**
5. **Outstanding**

**3 means the person did the job they were hired to do.** It is not a disappointing score, and treating it as one is the fastest way to make a scale meaningless. If most of your team sits at 4, the scale has stopped distinguishing anything and HR calibration will flatten it back out anyway.

## What the review already knows

OpenHRApp attaches an attendance and leave summary for the review period automatically — days present, late, absent and early out, an attendance percentage, and leave taken. You do not compile it and you cannot edit it.

Use it as context rather than as a rating. Attendance is a fact about a period; it is not a competency, and a strong contributor with a difficult few months should not be marked down twice for the same thing. Where it genuinely matters, say so explicitly in your comments rather than letting it silently depress a score.

## Writing the review

1. Open the review from the Performance screen.
2. Read the employee's self-assessment in full before rating anything.
3. Rate each competency and add comments.
4. Submit.

Once submitted it moves to HR and you can no longer edit it, so treat submission as final.

### Making the comments worth reading

The rating is a number; the comment is the only part anyone learns from. Three habits that carry most of the value:

- **Cite something specific.** "Handled the migration when the timeline moved twice" beats "adaptable". A rating without an example is unarguable and therefore unusable.
- **Address the self-assessment where you disagree.** If they rated themselves 5 and you gave 3, saying why in writing is the difference between a review and an ambush. They will read it before you speak.
- **Say what would change the score.** "Exceeds" and "meets" mean nothing without the gap being named.

## Common questions

### I disagree strongly with a self-assessment

Rate what you observed and explain the difference. A large gap is useful information for HR at calibration — it may reflect a person who undersells themselves, or an expectation that was never made explicit. Neither is resolved by quietly splitting the difference.

### Someone joined halfway through the cycle

Rate the period they were present and say so in the comments. The attendance summary covers the cycle dates, so a short tenure will show a low day count that is not their doing.

### I have not seen enough of their work

Say that, plainly, rather than defaulting everything to 3. A review that records "limited direct visibility this cycle" is honest and actionable. A page of unexplained middling scores is neither.

### Can I edit after submitting?

No. Contact HR before they finalise — they can raise it during calibration.
