Your self-assessment is the first stage of a performance review, and it is the only part where you set the terms. Your manager reads it before writing theirs, so what you put here shapes the conversation rather than just recording your side of it.

How the process works

A review passes through three people in a fixed order:

  1. You complete the self-assessment.
  2. Your manager adds their review — they cannot start until you have submitted.
  3. HR finalises it.

The ordering is deliberate. Your manager writes with your account in front of them, not the other way round, which is why leaving the self-assessment thin costs you something concrete: it is the only input that reaches them before they form a view.

Finding your review

Open Performance from the sidebar. A review appears when your organization opens a review cycle — you cannot start one yourself, and if nothing is listed, no cycle is currently open.

What you are rating

Six competencies by default:

  • Agility — adapting to changing priorities and handling change openly.
  • Collaboration
  • Customer Focus
  • Developing Others
  • Global Mindset
  • Innovation Mindset

Each carries a description and example behaviours. Read them — the behaviours are what you are actually being asked about, and rating from the one-word title means you and your manager may be answering different questions.

Your organization can change this set, so yours may differ.

The scale

  1. Needs Significant Improvement
  2. Below Expectations
  3. Meets Expectations
  4. Exceeds Expectations
  5. Outstanding

3 means you did the job you were hired to do. It is a good outcome, not a disappointing one. Rating yourself 5 everywhere is the most common self-assessment mistake and the least effective — it gives your manager nothing to agree with, and where they disagree the whole document loses credibility at once.

Writing it

  1. Open the review.
  2. Rate each competency and add comments.
  3. Submit.

Once submitted you cannot edit it, so finish before you send.

What makes a comment useful

The rating is a number your manager will probably adjust. The comment is the part that survives, and it is the only place you can put something they might not know.

  • Name specific work. "Rewrote the onboarding checklist after three new starters got stuck on the same step" is evidence. "Good at improving processes" is not.
  • Include what went wrong and what you did about it. A self-assessment that admits nothing reads as unreliable, and handling a problem well is often stronger evidence than avoiding one.
  • Say what you want next. This is the moment it gets read. A wish for different work, more responsibility, or a particular kind of training lands better here than raised informally later.
  • Mention things your manager cannot see. Helping another team, mentoring someone quietly, work that finished before they joined.

Your attendance is attached automatically

The review carries an attendance and leave summary for the cycle period — days present, late and absent, an attendance percentage, and leave taken. You do not enter it and cannot change it.

If there is context behind those numbers — a long illness, approved leave for a family matter, a period on a different shift — put it in your comments. The figures arrive without explanation otherwise, and your manager may be reading them without knowing what you told them months ago.

After you submit

The review moves to your manager, then to HR, who sets the final rating. You will be able to see your manager's feedback once the review is complete.

If your manager's rating differs sharply from yours, that gap is visible to HR at the final stage and is exactly the kind of thing calibration exists to look at — so a considered self-assessment is not wasted even when it is not agreed with.

Common questions

Can I edit after submitting?

No. Ask your manager or HR before the review is completed if something important was left out.

I joined part-way through the cycle

Say so in your comments. The attendance summary covers the whole cycle, so a short tenure shows a low day count that is not your doing.

Nothing appears under Performance

No review cycle is open. They are created by Admin or HR.

Do I have to rate myself at all?

Yes — the stage cannot be skipped, and a review with no self-assessment cannot move to your manager.
