When someone on your team requests leave, it arrives with you first. This guide covers what you are actually deciding, what happens after you decide it, and the one detail managers most often get wrong — that your approval is not the end of the process.

If you are approving on behalf of HR rather than as a line manager, see leave approval for HR instead; the final stage works differently.

Does my approval finalise the leave?

No. This is the most important thing on this page.

Approving a request moves it from Pending Manager to Pending HR. It is then waiting on HR or an administrator, who makes the final decision. Until that happens the leave is not approved, the employee's balance has not changed, and the days are not booked.

Rejection works the other way: a rejection from you is final. It does not go to HR for a second opinion. If you are unsure, it is better to ask the employee before rejecting than to reject and have them re-apply.

So the honest description of your step is: you are recommending approval and passing it on, or you are declining it outright.

Finding requests waiting on you

Open Leave from the sidebar. Requests needing your decision are listed as pending; you see them for the people you are responsible for, and nobody else's.

"The people you are responsible for" means something specific — members of teams where you are the leader, plus anyone whose line manager is set to you. It is not your department. If a request you expected never arrives, that mapping is the first thing to check.

What to look at before deciding

Each request shows the leave type, the start and end dates, the total days, the reason the employee gave, and when they applied.

Four things worth checking:

  • Does the team still function that week? You are the only person in the chain who knows this. HR can see the balance; they cannot see that two of your four people are already away.
  • Is the balance sufficient? Shown against the request. Note that pending requests are not deducted yet, so someone with two requests in flight can appear to have more remaining than they will end up with.
  • How much notice is this? A request for tomorrow is not automatically wrong, but it is worth a conversation.
  • Does it collide with a deadline or another absence?

Approving or rejecting

  1. Open the request.
  2. Add remarks. These are visible to the employee and to HR, and they travel with the request.
  3. Choose Approve or Reject.

The employee is notified either way, and an email goes out if your organization has leave notifications enabled — see notification settings.

Write remarks even when approving

Your remarks are the only record of why. HR sees the request next and has none of your context: they cannot tell whether you approved because cover was arranged or simply because the balance allowed it. One line — "cover arranged with Priya" — is the difference between a decision that can be understood later and one that cannot.

On rejection this matters more, because rejection is final and the employee has to decide what to do next. "Rejected" alone tells them nothing; "we have two people out that week, can you take the following week?" tells them exactly what to do.

Why did a request skip me?

Some requests go straight to HR without reaching you at all. Two reasons, both by design:

  • The department is configured to route to HR or Admin. Your organization can set an approval workflow per department that bypasses the manager stage entirely.
  • The employee has no line manager set. With nobody to route to, the request goes to HR rather than sitting unassigned.

Neither is an error, and neither is something you can change yourself. If you should be in the chain for a department that currently bypasses you, that is a conversation with your administrator — see understanding leave policies.

Common situations

The employee says their balance did not change after I approved

Correct, and expected. Balance is only reduced when leave reaches full approval. While a request sits at Pending HR, nothing has been deducted.

I approved by mistake

Contact HR before they action it. Once you have approved, the request is out of your hands — you cannot reverse your own decision, because the record of who decided what and when is the point of having stages at all.

The employee needs to change the dates

Reject the request with a remark explaining why, and ask them to submit the corrected dates. There is no way to edit someone else's request, deliberately: an approved request that says something different from what was submitted is not a reliable record.

Someone is already on leave and I never saw a request

Check whether it was routed past you for one of the reasons above, and check the dates — a request approved weeks ago is easy to forget.
