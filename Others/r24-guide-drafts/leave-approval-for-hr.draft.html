HR and Admin hold the final stage of every leave request. Nothing is approved until it passes through here, and this is also the only place leave can be created directly, corrected, or entered as a half day. This guide covers all three jobs.

Your stage is the one that counts

Requests arrive with you in one of two ways:

  • A manager approved it, which moved it to Pending HR rather than completing it.
  • It came straight to you, because the employee's department is configured to route to HR or Admin, or because they have no line manager set.

Either way, your approval is what makes the leave real — and it is the moment the employee's balance is reduced. Until you act, nothing has been deducted, which is why an employee can tell you their balance has not changed after their manager said yes. It has not, and that is correct.

Approving and rejecting

  1. Open Leave and find requests awaiting HR.
  2. Read the request and the manager's remarks.
  3. Add your own remarks.
  4. Approve or reject.

Read the manager's remarks before deciding rather than after. They are the only record of operational context — whether cover was arranged, whether the timing was discussed — and you cannot see any of it yourself.

Where you reject something a manager approved, say why explicitly. The manager has already told the employee it looked fine, and a reversal without explanation lands on them rather than on you.

Creating leave directly

HR and Admin can enter a leave record for an employee without a request. Use it for:

  • Leave agreed verbally or by email that was never entered.
  • Half days — the only route, since employee-submitted requests always come out in whole days.
  • Backdating an absence that should be recorded as leave rather than as an unexplained gap.
  • Statutory leave — maternity, paternity — that is arranged rather than requested.

When creating a record you set the type, the dates and the total days directly. The total days field accepts halves, so 0.5 is valid and is deducted as half a day.

Note that entering days manually means the automatic exclusion of weekends and holidays does not apply — you are stating the number, so check it against the calendar rather than counting the span.

Unblocking a stuck request

Requests sit at Pending Manager when the manager has not acted. Common causes, in order:

  • The manager does not know. If your organization has leave notifications disabled, requests arrive silently and rely on someone opening the screen.
  • The wrong person is set as line manager, so it is waiting with someone who has no idea why.
  • The manager is themselves away.

You can act on a request at any stage, so a genuinely urgent case does not have to wait. Use that sparingly — routinely approving past managers removes the one check that knows whether the team can cope, and managers stop watching a queue they have learned is handled without them.

Where a manager is repeatedly unavailable, changing the department workflow to route to HR is more honest than overriding case by case.

Corrections

An approved leave record that is wrong affects the employee's balance, so correct it rather than leaving it. Adjust the record itself; do not create a compensating second record, which leaves two entries that only make sense together.

What to check periodically

  • Requests sitting at Pending Manager for more than a few days. These are almost always invisible to the manager rather than being considered.
  • Employees with no line manager. Every one of them routes everything to you, which is fine until it is not.
  • Balances that look wrong. Usually a quota never set for a newly added leave type, which shows as zero available.
  • Absences recorded in attendance that should have been leave. These understate attendance and contradict the leave record.

Common questions

Can I approve my own leave?

Technically your role permits it. Whether you should is a policy question — most organizations route HR leave to a second approver, and doing so is worth agreeing before it comes up.

Does rejecting return the days?

Nothing was deducted, so there is nothing to return. Only approved leave affects a balance.

An employee says they never got a decision

Check whether your organization has leave notifications enabled. Decisions are recorded regardless, but without notifications the employee has to look.
