# Welcome to OpenHRApp — Your First Steps

OpenHRApp handles the administrative half of employing people: recording who worked when, managing time off, keeping employee records in one place, and running performance reviews. This guide is the map — what the app does, what you will see on your first day, and what to set up first if the organization is yours.

## What OpenHRApp does

- **Attendance** — employees clock in and out from their phone or computer, with a selfie and location recorded at the moment of the punch.
- **Leave** — requests, approvals and balances, with weekends and public holidays excluded from the count automatically.
- **Employee directory** — one record per person, holding the details everything else reads.
- **Performance reviews** — a three-stage cycle from self-assessment through manager review to HR sign-off.
- **Announcements** — a noticeboard targeted by role.
- **Reports** — attendance and leave turned into monthly figures, exportable as CSV or a branded PDF.

It is free, with no paid tier and no employee limit — see [subscription and upgrade options](/how-to-use/subscription-upgrade-options) for how that works.

## What you see depends on your role

OpenHRApp shows different screens to different roles, so your sidebar may be shorter than a colleague's and that is not a fault.

- **Employees** — their own attendance and leave, their team, announcements and reviews.
- **Managers** — the same, plus the people they lead and requests to approve.
- **HR and Admin** — the whole organization, plus configuration and reports.

See [roles and permissions](/how-to-use/roles-and-permissions) for what each one unlocks.

## Your first day as an employee

1. **Sign in** with the work email your organization registered.
2. **Check your profile.** Confirm your assigned shift and who approves your leave — both are on your dashboard, and both are easier to fix now than after a month of records.
3. **Clock in.** Grant camera and location permission when asked; they are requested once.
4. **Install the app** to your home screen if you clock in from a phone.

The one thing worth doing on day one is checking your shift. Lateness is measured against it, so a wrong shift quietly produces wrong records from the start.

## Setting up a new organization

If you have just registered, work in this order — each step depends on the one before:

1. **Departments**, under Organization & Setup.
2. **A shift** with your real working hours and a grace period you would actually enforce.
3. **Organization details** — name, timezone, and a logo, which appears on exported PDFs.
4. **This year's holidays**, which can be pre-populated for your country.
5. **Employees** — and as you add each one, set their **role, team, line manager and shift**.

Those four per-employee fields are the ones everything else reads. Skip them and the app appears to work while behaving oddly: leave routes to HR instead of managers, employees see nobody in the directory, and nothing is ever marked late.

Locations, custom leave types and approval routing can all wait until you need them.

## How the pieces connect

Most confusion comes from not knowing which setting drives which behaviour:

- Your **shift** decides what counts as late, and which days are working days for leave.
- Your **line manager** decides who approves your leave.
- Your **team** decides which colleagues you can see.
- Your **role** decides which screens you can open.
- The **holiday calendar** decides which days a leave request does not consume.

When something behaves unexpectedly, one of those five is usually the answer.
