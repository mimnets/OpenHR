Applying for leave takes about a minute. This guide covers the steps, how OpenHRApp works out how many days you are actually using, and how to read the status your request sits in afterwards — which is where most questions come from.

Check your balance first

Your remaining days per leave type are on your dashboard. Two things to know before you rely on that number:

  • Only fully approved leave has been deducted. A request still working through approval is not counted yet, so if you have something pending, your true remaining figure is lower than the one shown.
  • Not every type has a balance. Annual, casual and sick leave count down. Maternity, paternity, earned and unpaid leave are recorded without a quota, so they show no remaining figure — that is correct, not a fault.

Submitting a request

  1. Open Leave from the sidebar.
  2. Start a new request.
  3. Choose the leave type.
  4. Pick your start and end dates.
  5. Write a reason.
  6. Submit.

How many days will this actually cost me?

Fewer than the calendar span, usually. OpenHRApp counts only the days you would otherwise have worked, and excludes two kinds of day automatically:

  • Your non-working days — from your shift if you have one, otherwise your organization's working-day setting.
  • Public holidays — from your organization's calendar.

You see the breakdown as you pick the dates — how many weekends and how many holidays were excluded — so a request spanning a long weekend shows exactly what it will use. You never lose allowance to a day you were not going to work anyway, and you do not have to work it out yourself.

Writing a reason worth reading

Your reason is read by your approver and stays attached to the request permanently. It does not need to be detailed or personal — "family commitment" is a complete reason — but it should say enough for someone to decide without asking you.

Where timing matters, say so. "Flights already booked" or "medical appointment, cannot move" tells an approver something the dates alone do not, and it is the difference between a quick approval and a conversation.

Where does my request go?

Straight to one of two places, decided automatically:

  • To your line manager, in the normal case.
  • Straight to HR, if your department is configured to route that way, or if you have no line manager set.

Your dashboard shows who approves your requests. If that is not who you expect, your line manager is set wrong — get it corrected before submitting, because routing is decided when the request is created and cannot be redirected afterwards.

Reading the status

  • Pending Manager — waiting on your manager.
  • Pending HR — your manager has approved and passed it on. Not yet approved.
  • Approved — final. Your balance is reduced at this point, and not before.
  • Rejected — declined. A manager's rejection is final and does not go to HR for a second opinion.

The stage that surprises people is Pending HR. Your manager saying yes is genuinely good news, but the leave is not booked and your balance has not moved. Do not treat it as confirmed until it reads Approved.

Common questions

Can I edit or cancel a request?

No. Ask your approver to reject it, with a note about why, and submit a corrected one. That is deliberate: a request that can be edited after approval is not a reliable record of what was agreed.

Can I apply for half a day?

Not directly. Dates you choose produce whole days. Half days exist, but only Admin or HR can enter one on your behalf — so ask, rather than booking a full day for a morning appointment.

My balance did not change after my manager approved

Expected. Deduction happens at final approval, not at the manager stage.

My request has sat unactioned for days

Check the status. At Pending Manager, chase your manager; at Pending HR, chase HR. If your organization has leave notifications disabled, your approver may genuinely not know it is there — the request appears on their screen but arrives silently.

I need leave that overlaps a holiday

Just request the full span. The holiday is excluded from the count automatically.
