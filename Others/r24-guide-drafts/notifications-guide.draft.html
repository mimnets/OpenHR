Notifications tell you when something needs you — a leave request to approve, a decision on your own request, a review waiting, an announcement posted. This guide covers your side of them: reading, muting, and working out why one did or did not arrive.

For the organization-wide settings an administrator controls, see notification settings.

The notification bell

The bell in the top bar carries a count of unread notifications. Open it to read them, mark them read, and set your own preferences — this is where your personal controls live, not on the Settings page.

What generates a notification

Notifications fall into five categories, and each is triggered by a specific event:

  • Leave — a request submitted to you as approver; a decision made on your own request.
  • Attendance — check-in reminders, late alerts, and notice when a forgotten session was closed automatically.
  • Performance reviews — cycle updates, and a review reaching your stage.
  • Announcements — a new announcement targeted at your role.
  • System — administrative and maintenance messages.

Which of these you actually receive depends on your role. An employee never gets leave-approval notifications because nothing routes to them for approval; a manager gets them for their own people only.

Muting what you do not need

Open the bell and mute any category individually. It applies to you alone and changes nothing for anyone else.

This is the right tool when a category is noisy for you personally. Asking an administrator to disable it organization-wide is not — that removes it for people who were relying on it, including approvers who will simply stop being told about requests.

Two categories are worth thinking twice about before muting: Leave, if you approve anything, and Performance reviews during a cycle. Both carry things that stall silently when nobody looks.

Email

You can also set how often you receive email — immediately, a daily digest, a weekly digest, or not at all.

A digest is usually the right answer for anyone getting more than a handful a day. It reduces the volume without losing anything, because everything still appears on the bell as it happens.

Setting email to off is not the same as muting. Off stops email only — notifications keep arriving in the app. If you want to stop seeing a category entirely, mute the category.

Why did I not get notified?

Work down this list; the first two cover most cases.

  • Your organization has that category disabled. An administrator can switch off whole categories, and no personal setting overrides that. Nothing is sent to anyone.
  • You muted it. Check the bell.
  • You are on a digest. On weekly, an email about something from Tuesday may legitimately be days away. The bell shows it immediately regardless.
  • Quiet hours covered the time. Your organization can suppress notifications during a set window.
  • Your work email is out of date — check it on your profile.
  • It was never sent to you. Announcements are targeted at specific roles; leave notifications go to the approver the request routed to, which may not be who you expect.

Why did I get notified about something that is not mine?

Usually because you are set as somebody's line manager, or lead a team they are in, without realising. Both make their requests route to you. Your dashboard shows who you are responsible for.

How long are notifications kept?

They are cleared automatically after a retention period so the list stays a to-do rather than an archive.

The practical consequence: do not use the bell as a record. Approvals, attendance and review outcomes are all stored in their own sections and are not affected when a notification disappears. If you need to find what happened to a leave request from three months ago, look at the leave records, not the bell.

Common questions

Can I get push notifications on my phone?

Installing OpenHRApp to your home screen gives it a place to deliver them. See installing OpenHRApp.

Can I forward notifications to a shared inbox?

Email goes to the work email on your profile. For a shared approval queue, set that address on the account that approves.

Marking everything read — does it delete them?

No, it only clears the count. They disappear on their own when the retention period passes.
